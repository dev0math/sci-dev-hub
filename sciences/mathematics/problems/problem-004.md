# Problem 004

**Source:** IMO 1988, Problem 6 (Canberra, Australia) — proposed by West Germany

**Difficulty:** ★★★★★

**Field / Subfield:** Number Theory — Vieta Jumping / Infinite Descent

**Keywords / Tags:** Vieta jumping, infinite descent, perfect squares, Diophantine equations

---

## Problem Statement

Let $a$ and $b$ be positive integers such that $ab + 1$ divides $a^2 + b^2$.

Prove that

$$
\frac{a^2 + b^2}{ab + 1}
$$

is a perfect square of an integer.

---

## Notes

This problem is historically significant as the origin of the "Vieta jumping" (root-flipping) technique, now a standard tool in olympiad number theory. It is widely regarded as one of the hardest problems ever to appear on the IMO — reportedly, of the contestants who attempted it that year, only a small handful scored full marks despite it being the final problem of the exam.
